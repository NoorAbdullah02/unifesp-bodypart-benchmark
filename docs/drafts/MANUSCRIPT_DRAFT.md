Working title: *How reliable are radiograph body-part classifiers? A leakage-controlled, cross-dataset benchmark on public data.*

## Abstract (skeleton)
**Background:** Automatic identification of the anatomical region in radiographs supports PACS routing and cohort building, yet published results are hard to compare because splits, metrics and thresholds are rarely reported rigorously.
**Methods:** We built a leakage-controlled benchmark on the public UNIFESP body-part dataset (22 classes, 1,738 labelled radiographs, patient-level folds, a fixed held-out set, all decisions made on out-of-fold predictions, patient-level bootstrap confidence intervals) and tested transfer to MURA and COVID-19 Radiography **[RESULT]**.
**Results:** The ensemble reached a sample-mean F1 of 0.919 (95 % CI 0.867-0.960) on 323 held-out images and 0.9256 on the competition's private test score (the finished leaderboard's #1 is 0.9338). The ensemble was not significantly better than its best single member (+0.0066, p = 0.063). **[RESULT: ablations, generalization, explainability]**

## 1 Introduction (draft)
Radiographs enter hospital archives with the anatomical region encoded in DICOM metadata or free-text study descriptions, and these fields are often wrong or missing: one early audit reported the *Body Part Examined* tag to be incorrect in 15.3 % of cases (Güld et al., cited through Dratsch et al. 2019) **[VERIFY: original paper not opened]**. Deep-learning classifiers can recover the region from pixels (Pham et al. 2021; Langer et al. 2024; Eur Radiol 2025 multi-institutional study) **[VERIFY numbers]**, but most large studies use private data, report accuracy only, and say little about how splits and thresholds were chosen.
On the public UNIFESP dataset (Farina, Abdala, Kitamura 2022) we found one academic paper, which reports an accuracy of 86.94 % for five CNNs (Krishnan & Padmavathi 2025) **[VERIFY: split and metric definition]**, and a Kaggle competition whose best private score is 0.93378.
Evaluation shortcuts known from other fields - data leakage (Kapoor & Narayanan 2023; Roberts et al. 2021) and thresholds tuned on the scored data - can inflate such numbers.
**Contributions** (state only those backed by finished experiments): (i) a leakage-controlled, reproducible benchmark on public data; (ii) a measurement of how much common evaluation shortcuts inflate scores **[RESULT]**; (iii) a negative result on gated CNN-Transformer fusion and anatomical hierarchical loss **[RESULT]**; (iv) cross-dataset transfer between UNIFESP, MURA and FracAtlas through an explicit class map **[RESULT]**; (v) an explainability audit with faithfulness and randomisation checks **[RESULT]**.

## 2 Related work (draft outline)
* **Body-part classification:** Pham 2021 (16,093 images, 5 groups); Langer 2024 (48,434 hospital radiographs, 14 regions; an expert found 35 % of test-error labels wrong and 11 % out-of-domain); Eur Radiol 2025 (> 860,000 radiographs, external accuracy 98.5 %); Shah 2026 (MURA 7-class, Xception-Swin, acc 0.9643, one seed, no CI) **[VERIFY all]**.
* **Hybrid CNN-Transformer fusion:** gated or attention-fused CNN+Swin/ViT models are common in the literature (e.g. ConvNeXt+Swin with dual-attention gating, EfficientNetV2+Swin with gated fusion); we did not find a report analysing gate saturation **[VERIFY: absence not proof]**.
* **Hierarchical losses:** disease hierarchies (Asadi 2025, CheXpert); a hierarchical loss can hurt on non-hierarchical problems (Wu, Tygert, LeCun 2019).
* **Evaluation:** leakage (Kapoor & Narayanan 2023), dataset label quality (Oakden-Rayner), reporting guidance (CLAIM 2024).

## 3 Data
**UNIFESP** (Farina, Abdala, Kitamura 2022; Kaggle, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): 2,481 DICOM radiographs, 20 body regions plus "Others", multilabel annotation by radiology residents. 1,738 training images from 768 patients (63 % of patients have more than one image) and 743 test images from 330 patients whose labels are hidden; no patient appears in both. All images are 256 x 256 pixels; 2,367 (95 %) are MONOCHROME1 (inverted polarity) and 114 MONOCHROME2; BitsStored is 15 (2,367 images), 8 (69), 10 (43) or 12 (2). The DICOM *BodyPartExamined* tag is empty in **all 2,481 files**, so the classifier cannot rely on metadata and the tag-error rate cannot be measured on this dataset.
92.4 % of training images carry exactly one label (122 two labels, 10 three). Class frequencies range from 738 (Chest, 42.5 %) to 9 (Clavicles): an 82-fold imbalance. The most frequent label pairs are Lumbar + Thoracic Spine (48 images) and Hand + Wrist (42). Several images contain more than one view side by side (e.g. ankle, feet, hand, wrist panels), and the "Others" class is heterogeneous (Figure: montage; a low-quality spine image appears among the first "Others" examples).
**Splits.** The 1,738 images are split at patient level with multilabel stratification (iterative stratification, seed 42): a fixed held-out set of 323 images / 156 patients (20 %, never used for training, model selection, ensemble weights or thresholds) and a CV pool of 1,415 images / 612 patients in 5 folds (269-307 images each). The held-out set has 1-6 positives for Clavicles (1), Skull (2), Forearm (3), Elbow (3), Thigh (3), so per-class numbers for these classes are unstable.
**External datasets [RESULT when run]:** MURA body-part (7 classes taken from the `XR_*` folder names; official train/validation protocol so that results are comparable with Shah et al. 2026), COVID-19 Radiography (4 classes; no patient identifiers, so the split is image-level - a stated limitation), FracAtlas (4,083 images, regions hand/leg/hip/shoulder, CC BY 4.0) for zero-shot transfer **[VERIFY region columns]**.

## 4 Methods
**Pre-processing.** Rescale slope/intercept, inversion of MONOCHROME1 images, per-image min-max normalisation to 8 bit, replication to 3 channels, resize to 224 (Swin-T) or 256 pixels (native size), ImageNet mean/std.
**Models.** ImageNet-pretrained timm backbones (Swin-T, DenseNet121, EfficientNetV2-S, ConvNeXt-T, ResNet50, EfficientNet-B3), each with two linear heads: 22 sigmoid logits (multilabel) and 22 softmax logits (single-label view of the task). Fusion variants for the ablation: DenseNet121 + Swin-T with a learned gate on raw features, gate with LayerNorm, fixed 0.5/0.5 average, CNN only, Swin only.
**Training.** AdamW (weight decay 1e-4; lr 2e-4 Swin, 3e-4 DenseNet/ResNet/EfficientNet, 1e-4 ConvNeXt), cosine schedule with one warm-up epoch, 30 epochs, batch 32, fp16 autocast, EMA of the weights (decay 0.998), gradient clipping 5. Loss = BCE-with-logits with positive weights capped at 20 + 0.5 x soft-target cross-entropy on the softmax head. Augmentation: horizontal flip, shift-scale-rotate, brightness/contrast ("light") plus Gaussian noise/blur ("heavy"). **No checkpoint selection:** the final EMA weights are used, so out-of-fold predictions are unbiased.
**Decisions on out-of-fold (OOF) predictions only:** the head/TTA variant of each run (sigmoid, softmax or their blend, with/without horizontal-flip TTA), the ensemble weights (Caruana greedy selection, 40 iterations), the per-class decision thresholds (coordinate ascent on the sample-mean F1, shrunk toward 0.5 with weight n_pos/(n_pos+20)), and the "at least one label per image" rule. The held-out set is used once, to report.
**Metrics and statistics.** Sample-mean F1 (the competition metric: F1 per image, averaged), macro-F1, micro-F1, subset accuracy; 95 % confidence intervals by patient-level bootstrap (2,000 resamples); paired patient bootstrap for model comparisons.
**Hardware/software [FILL]:** Kaggle notebooks, 2 x NVIDIA T4, PyTorch 2.11 (cu130 as printed by the notebook), timm and albumentations versions from the run logs; code, split files and seeds to be released (GitHub + Zenodo DOI).

## 5 Results
**5.1 Main result (UNIFESP, held-out set).** Ensemble of six runs, thresholds tuned on OOF: sample-mean F1 0.9189 [0.8669, 0.9602], macro-F1 0.8630 [0.6891, 0.8916], micro-F1 0.9148, subset accuracy 0.8885. At threshold 0.5: 0.9164 / 0.8537. Best single run by OOF (ConvNeXt-T): 0.9123. Ensemble minus best single: +0.0066 [-0.0002, +0.0142], p = 0.063. (Table 1.)
**5.2 Competition score.** Late submission of the stage-1 ensemble: private 0.92563, public 0.92704; finished private leaderboard #1 0.93378, #2 0.92787, #3 0.92488. The private score uses about 60 % of the test set with the original (partly noisy) labels; the standard error of such a score is about 0.012 (our calculation), so the gap to #1 (0.0082) is not statistically resolvable. **[RESULT: stage-2 scores; report every submission made.]**

## 6 Discussion (points to make once results are in)
Why the ensemble helps little (members agree; diversity from heavy augmentation gets the weight); where errors concentrate (rare classes with 1-6 held-out positives; the heterogeneous "Others" class); label noise and the ceiling (competition hosts re-annotated the test set offline); what the gate collapse means for hybrid-model claims; clinical use (flagging suspicious metadata, not replacing it).
## 7 Limitations
Single-centre dataset; 323 held-out images and rare classes; COVID split is image-level; FracAtlas class map assumed; private test labels noisy; stage-2 uses unlabelled test images (transductive); Grad-CAM is coarse and not validated against localisation labels or radiologist review.
