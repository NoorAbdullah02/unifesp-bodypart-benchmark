Figure 1. One example radiograph per class from the UNIFESP dataset (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), with the number of training images in each class. Several images show more than one view side by side.

Figure 2. Evaluation protocol. Images are split by patient into a cross-validation pool and a held-out set. Every choice is made on out-of-fold predictions, and the held-out set is scored once, with patient-bootstrap confidence intervals.

Figure 3. (A) Training images per class, on a log scale. (B) Number of labels per image.

Figure 4. (A) The ten most frequent label pairs; two pairs, Lumbar with Thoracic Spine and Hand with Wrist, account for most multilabel images. (B) Images per class in the cross-validation pool and in the held-out set, on a log scale.

Figure 5. The models and the decision pipeline. Six pretrained backbones, each with a sigmoid and a softmax head, are trained on five patient-level folds. Head blending, ensemble weights, thresholds and the minimum-one-label rule are all chosen on out-of-fold predictions.

Figure 6. Held-out sample-F1 for a frozen DINOv2-S probe, the fine-tuned Swin-T, the best single model and the ensemble. All 95 % confidence intervals overlap.

Figure 7. (A) How much a random image-level split and tuning thresholds on the held-out set overstate the score. (B) Mean weight the learned fusion gates give the CNN branch, compared with a fixed average.

Figure 8. Change in held-out sample-F1 against the reference Swin-T for each ablation (95 % paired patient-bootstrap interval; p-values on the right). None of the intervals excludes zero.

Figure 9. (A) Label efficiency of frozen encoders. (B) Held-out F1 of the frozen probes next to the fine-tuned models.

Figure 10. (A) Accuracy on the images the model answers, as a function of coverage. (B) Accuracy targets chosen on out-of-fold predictions, and the accuracy reached on the held-out set.

Figure 11. (A) The pairs of given and suggested labels among the 39 suspected label errors. (B) The most frequent misclassifications of the three explained models on the held-out set.

Figure 12. (A) Body-part accuracy on MURA (seven classes), compared with the models reported by Shah et al. (2026). (B) Zero-shot accuracy through the class map, for the softmax and sigmoid heads; intervals come from an image-level bootstrap and are optimistic.

Figure 13. Grad-CAM galleries for ConvNeXt-T (left), ResNet50 (middle) and Swin-T (right). For each class there is one correct and one missed held-out image, with the map for the true class and for the predicted class.
